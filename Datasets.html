About this explorer

This app overlays three satellite sensors — Landsat 9, Sentinel-2, and MODIS — on the same basin, so surface conditions can be compared across resolutions and revisit frequencies. Toggle layers inside the map to switch between true-colour composites and the MODIS NDVI time series.

How to use it
  • Toggle the layer checkboxes inside the map to switch between sensors.
  • Zoom into Pune city to compare Sentinel-2's fine detail against Landsat's broader view.
  • Use the MODIS NDVI layer to read basin-wide vegetation trends across the monsoon cycle.
  • Switch between the coloured tabs here for band and dataset details on each sensor.

Interpretation

Sentinel-2's 10–20 m resolution resolves field boundaries, riverbanks, and built-up patches within Pune city, making it the most useful layer for spotting localized surface conditions relevant to flood-prone zones. Landsat 9, at 30 m, offers a coarser but historically consistent cross-check. MODIS NDVI, at 250 m and a 16-day revisit, is too coarse for parcel-level detail but well suited to tracking basin-wide vegetation response across the monsoon cycle — its time series shows a clear seasonal rise and fall through pre-monsoon, monsoon, and post-monsoon periods. Together, these products show how scale choice shapes what can and cannot be observed in flood hazard analysis.

Landsat 9

USGS / NASA — 30 m, 16-day revisit

Collection: LANDSAT/LC09/C02/T1_L2, Surface Reflectance Level-2. Cloud and shadow pixels are removed using the QA_PIXEL band before compositing.

SR_B4 — Red SR_B3 — Green SR_B2 — Blue

Bands are scaled (×0.0000275, offset −0.2) and rendered as a true-colour composite, min 0 / max 0.3. Landsat's 30 m resolution and multi-decade archive make it a coarser but historically consistent cross-check against the finer Sentinel-2 layer — useful for confirming long-term surface trends rather than parcel-level detail.

Sentinel-2

ESA / Copernicus — 10 m, 5-day revisit

Collection: COPERNICUS/S2_SR_HARMONIZED, Surface Reflectance. The Scene Classification Layer (SCL) masks cloud and cloud-shadow pixels before compositing.

B4 — Red B3 — Green B2 — Blue

Bands are scaled (×0.0001) and rendered as a true-colour composite, min 0 / max 0.3. At 10 m, this is the sharpest layer in the app — sharp enough to resolve field boundaries, riverbanks, and individual built-up patches across Pune, making it the most useful layer for spotting localized surface conditions.

MODIS

NASA Terra — 250 m, 16-day composite

Collection: MODIS/061/MOD13Q1, Vegetation Indices. Pixels are filtered using the SummaryQA band, keeping only good and marginal quality retrievals.

NDVI

NDVI is scaled (×0.0001) and rendered on a red-to-green palette, min 0 / max 1. Far too coarse for parcel-level reading, MODIS instead tracks basin-wide vegetation response over time — the NDVI series shows a clear seasonal rise and fall through the pre-monsoon, monsoon, and post-monsoon periods.
